1. Angles, degrees, and radians - PDF pages 3-7
Angle
Two rays meeting at a common endpoint
The common endpoint is the vertex.
Degree
1 complete revolution=360°
The degree symbol is °.
Radian
1 complete revolution=2π rad
Radians are another unit for measuring angles.
Key Equivalence
180°=π radians
This equality creates both conversion factors.
Degrees to Radians
radians=degrees×π/180
60°×π/180=π/3.
Radians to Degrees
degrees=radians×180/π
(π/4)×180/π=45°.

2. Sine, cosine, tangent, and reciprocal ratios - PDF pages 8-10
SOH
sin θ=opposite/hypotenuse
Use sine when opposite and hypotenuse are involved.
CAH
cos θ=adjacent/hypotenuse
Use cosine when adjacent and hypotenuse are involved.
TOA
tan θ=opposite/adjacent
Use tangent when opposite and adjacent are involved.
Cosecant
csc θ=1/sin θ
The reciprocal of sine.
Secant
sec θ=1/cos θ
The reciprocal of cosine.
Cotangent
cot θ=1/tan θ
The reciprocal of tangent.
Side names depend on the reference angle θ
Important: Opposite and adjacent depend on which acute angle is selected as θ. The hypotenuse never changes.
3. Right-triangle trigonometry - PDF pages 11-13
Right Triangle
Exactly one angle is 90°
The other two angles are acute.
Complementary Acute Angles
Angle A+Angle B=90°
A triangle totals 180°.
Pythagorean Theorem
c²=a²+b²
c is the hypotenuse.
Find the Hypotenuse
c=√(a²+b²)
For legs 3 and 4, c=5.
Find Leg a
a=√(c²−b²)
Subtract the square of the known leg.
Find Leg b
b=√(c²−a²)
The hypotenuse must be the value used for c.
Mark the right angle and identify the hypotenuse.
Choose the reference angle θ.
Label the opposite and adjacent sides relative to θ.
Choose SOH, CAH, TOA, or the Pythagorean Theorem.
Substitute values, solve, and check whether the answer is reasonable.
